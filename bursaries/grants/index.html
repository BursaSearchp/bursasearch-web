University grants UK: money you never pay back

A grant is money for university you never pay back. UK students can get them from four places: their university (2,792 grants, bursaries and scholarships across 148 unis), hardship funds (171), charities and trusts (1,039), and the government. Most students only ever hear about the student loan.

    Government grants and allowances

    Official help on top of your student loan. Tap one to see who gets it and how to apply.

    Childcare Grant (England)

    Who: Full-time higher education students with children under 15, or under 17 with special educational needs, in registered or approved childcare.

    Pays up to 85% of your childcare costs, up to a weekly cap that depends on household income and how many children you have. Paid on top of your student finance and never paid back. Apply through Student Finance England.

    Official page →

    Parents' Learning Allowance (England)

    Who: Full-time students with dependent children.

    Helps with course costs like books and travel. The amount depends on household income. It isn't paid back, comes on top of your student finance and doesn't affect your benefits. Apply through Student Finance England.

    Official page →

    Adult Dependants' Grant (England)

    Who: Full-time students with an adult, usually a partner, who depends on them financially.

    Not paid back and paid on top of your student finance, though it can affect income-related benefits. Apply through Student Finance England.

    Official page →

    Disabled Students' Allowance (UK)

    Who: Undergraduates and postgraduates with a disability, long-term illness, mental health condition or a specific learning difficulty such as dyslexia.

    Pays the study costs your condition causes: specialist equipment, non-medical helpers like note takers, and taxis if you can't use public transport. Based on your needs, not household income, and never paid back. Apply through your student finance body; a needs assessment decides what you get.

    Official page →

    NHS Learning Support Fund (England)

    Who: Students on eligible nursing, midwifery and allied health courses, such as physiotherapy, radiography and paramedic science.

    A yearly training grant you don't pay back, extra money if you have children, help with placement travel and accommodation, and an exceptional support fund if you hit hardship. Apply through the NHS Business Services Authority.

    Official page →

    Social Work Bursary (England)

    Who: Social work students in England, undergraduate and postgraduate.

    A bursary you don't pay back. Undergraduate places are limited and your university nominates who gets one. Run by the NHS Business Services Authority.

    Official page →

    Teacher training bursaries and scholarships (England)

    Who: Trainee teachers on postgraduate teacher training in shortage subjects such as maths, physics, chemistry and computing.

    Tax-free money you don't pay back, set each year by subject. The current subject list is on Get Into Teaching.

    Official page →

    Welsh Government Learning Grant (Wales)

    Who: Students from Wales on full-time or part-time undergraduate courses.

    A grant towards living costs that you don't pay back. The amount depends on household income. Apply through Student Finance Wales.

    Official page →

    Young Students' Bursary (Scotland)

    Who: Scottish students under 25 when their course starts, from lower-income households.

    Not paid back; the amount depends on household income. Students 25 or over, or who support themselves, can get the Independent Students' Bursary instead. Apply through SAAS.

    Official page →

    Care Experienced Students' Bursary (Scotland)

    Who: Care-experienced Scottish students.

    A bursary for living costs in place of a student loan, so you don't build up debt for them. Apply through SAAS.

    Official page →

    Maintenance Grant (Northern Ireland)

    Who: Students from Northern Ireland from lower-income households.

    Not paid back; the amount depends on household income. Apply through Student Finance NI.

    Official page →

    Higher education bursary for care leavers (England)

    Who: Care leavers in England starting a degree.

    Your local council pays a £2,000 bursary on top of anything your university gives. Ask your personal adviser. Propel lists what every university offers care leavers.

    Official page →

    Independent student status (UK)

    Who: Students estranged from their parents.

    Student finance can assess you as independent, so your parents' income isn't counted. That usually means the biggest loan and the biggest income-based bursaries. Stand Alone explains the evidence you need.

    Official page →

    Questions

    What grants can university students get in the UK?

    Grants and bursaries from your own university (148 UK universities run them), hardship funds for when money runs short, grants from charities and trusts, and government grants such as the Childcare Grant, Parents' Learning Allowance, Adult Dependants' Grant and Disabled Students' Allowance.

    Do I have to pay a grant back?

    No. Grants, bursaries and scholarships are not loans. You don't repay them.

    Are there grants for students from low-income families?

    Yes. Most universities pay an income-based bursary to students from lower-income households, often automatically through your Student Finance assessment, and many charity grants are aimed at low income too.
